# Bremen synthetic missing-input stress test v1

Locally fixed after all prior IDEAL and Bremen results and before computing this stress experiment. Exploratory post-result analysis, not public preregistration or independent verification. It investigates sensitivity to specified synthetic missingness, not real outages or a population of sites.

## Inputs, masked signals, and timing
Use the pinned four audited Bremen hourly series and the same fixed IDEAL development-source ridge, monthly decision windows and six-hour target defined by protocol_bremen_fallback_v1.md. Verify their local SHA-256, source cohort, 55 homes and 125,779 rows. Apply the artificial mask to the entire target-room hourly series from 2023-01-25 00 UTC to 2024-01-01 00 UTC, including selector-history measurements and historical labels; a masked measurement cannot supply an input or a historical target. Preserve naturally missing values. The original, unmasked quality-filtered target at t+6 is retained solely as an oracle for retrospective scoring, not supplied to the forecasting policy. It is a counterfactual benchmark assuming an independent way to recover/observe truth; in a real single-sensor outage the target itself may be unavailable and scoring may be delayed or impossible. Do not mask test scoring targets, as doing so obscures oracle comparisons; do not infer real-world scored coverage. Hourly inputs are available only after the labelled hour ends; transmission and reconstruction latency not modeled.

## Fixed scenarios
1. none: no synthetic mask, one replicate; must reproduce original saved baseline metrics to numeric tolerance.
2. iid05, iid20: independent hourly deletion probabilities 0.05 and 0.20 respectively.
3. block6h_7d, block24h_14d, block72h_30d: contiguous repeating blocks of length 6, 24 and 72 hourly bins with periods 168, 336 and 720 hours, respectively. For each room/replicate use a uniform random phase within the period. Blocks continue through monthly boundaries. These patterns are idealized and not fitted to observed failures. Five reproducible replicates per nonzero scenario. Use NumPy default_rng seed `20261029 + 1000*scenario_index + 10*replicate_index + room_index`, scenario indices 1–5, replicates 0–4, rooms in South_2, South_5, South_8, South_9 order (indices 0–3). Room masks independent by seed; results do not model common infrastructure outages.

## Procedure and output
Recompute selector choice for each room/month using only complete masked history with target-hour start strictly before month start, a 30-day lookback, >=24 complete samples, and fixed candidate/tie order. Refit local ridge from the same masked history. Selector and source need all t,t-1,t-18 features; selector additionally needs history. Route selector then persistence then daily then abstain, exactly as the Bremen baseline. Enumerate all eligible issue times regardless of future target availability. Record readiness per method, branches, histories and choices, natural and synthetic missingness and oracle-target availability. Evaluate MAE only where forecast issued and unmasked original target exists. For fallback vs persistence, compare MAE only on their paired scored support, equal room weights. Also report extra issued/scored fallback predictions outside persistence support and their MAE separately; never claim their own-support error proves superiority. Report scenario/replicate-level values and ranges; no statistical significance or population confidence intervals from the replicates or correlated rooms. Pair comparisons to none descriptively; changing masks changes sample composition. A common support across stress and no-outage conditions is a secondary descriptive sensitivity, and differences include model-selection changes due to masked history.

Baseline comparisons must pass before producing outputs. Save replicate, room, choices and branch tables plus summary and protocol hash; refuse to overwrite prior outputs. Preserve all prior artifacts. Stress is synthetic, added after the initial results, not independent validation, no energy, comfort, climate transfer or controller conclusions.
